Who Owns Tower? · The Queens business

Tower Auto Mall and the leasing network

A used-car and livery dealer on Queens Boulevard dating to 2006, a leasing company its president manages, and weekly-payment contracts with Uber drivers documented in 2016 and 2023.

Research cutoff October 21, 2026 · Documented court records, published reporting, business profiles

The company whose president manages CarToo in Los Angeles has been a car dealer in Queens since 2006, trading as a livery center. What its driver-financing business looks like from the driver's side is on the public record in two places: a 2017 investigation describing a 2016 contract, and a 2025 federal opinion describing a 2023 application. None of it is a finding against any individual. All of it describes the product.

Tower Auto Mall

Documented Tower Auto Mall, Inc. is a used- and new-car dealer at 3110 Queens Boulevard, Long Island City, in business since May 2006, with a second location in the Bronx. It also trades as Tower West and Tower West Livery Center Corp. The BBB lists Ariel Tzur as president and Rafael Tsour as CEO. "Livery" is the New York term for for-hire vehicles, the cars that drive for Uber and the city's black-car bases under Taxi and Limousine Commission plates. A livery center sells and leases those cars to the drivers who operate them.

The 2017 Quartz investigation

Documented In June 2017, Quartz published an investigation by Alison Griswold into Uber's practice of referring New York drivers to third-party dealers for vehicles. Tower Auto Mall was one of four dealers Uber recommended. The piece follows a driver, Geovanie Rosario, who went to Tower in May 2016 and signed for a used Lincoln MKS:

TermAmount
Up-front service fee$3,000
Weekly payment$495
Number of weeks159
Scheduled payments plus fee$81,705
CollectionDeducted from Uber earnings every Monday

Quartz identified CB Livery Leasing LLC as the financing company on the contract and Tower Auto Mall as the dealer and servicer. Rosario told the reporter that about a month in he got pneumonia, fell behind, and found the car remotely disabled; when he called, he was told, "You have to make a payment." That is his account as reported, not a court finding, and the article does not say who at the company made the decision. Uber told Quartz it was halting referrals to its New York third-party leasing program pending an audit. That was a program-wide pause, not a ban on Tower.

CB Livery Leasing and Ariel Tzur

Documented Two court records connect Tzur to the leasing company in the Quartz story. In a February 2023 affidavit in a Kings County case (Sudduth v. Winfield, Index 510474/2022), Tzur identified himself as manager of CB Livery Leasing LLC, said the company had leased and rented vehicles for more than eight years from several locations, and gave 1415 Cromwell Avenue in the Bronx as its principal office. We have seen the affidavit as reproduced by a court-records service; the original on the state's e-filing system is a verification target. In progress

In Leiba v. CB Livery Leasing LLC (Sup. Ct. N.Y. County, Index 151733/2023, decided March 11, 2026), the court refers to Tzur as a principal of CB Livery and discusses his affidavit about a leased vehicle's maintenance. CB Livery had asked to be dismissed from an injury case under the federal Graves Amendment, which shields vehicle lessors from liability based on ownership alone. The court denied the motion because the company's submissions did not adequately document the vehicle's maintenance. That is a ruling that the company had not yet proved its entitlement to summary judgment. It is not a finding that the company was negligent or that Tzur did anything wrong.

The 2025 federal case

Documented In Manuela v. Tower Auto Mall, Inc. (E.D.N.Y. No. 1:24-cv-07159, Nov. 5, 2025), a driver alleged that he applied in October 2023 to finance a 2022 Chevrolet Suburban on a form with a section headed "This Section For Uber Lease To Own Customers Only," paid a $1,000 down payment, was denied, and never got the deposit back or an explanation. The court dismissed his federal claims, with leave to amend two of them, and made no finding that Tower Auto Mall violated any law. What the opinion does supply is the court's description of the business, made while explaining why Tower Auto Mall is not a "bank" under the Federal Reserve Act: a dealer that "accepts no deposits and only provides loans for the purchase of its own goods." ("Deposits" there means bank deposits, not the driver's down payment.) The same opinion records a lease-to-own application form for Uber drivers in use in 2023, seven years after the contract Quartz reported.

The affiliated leasing companies

Documented A 2024 Queens County commercial case, Tower Auto Mall, Inc. et al. v. Ben-Nany et al. (Index 713108/2024), lists five plaintiffs together: Tower Auto Mall, Inc.; Tower Management of N.Y., Inc.; CB Livery Leasing LLC; QLR Five LLC; and Venture Leasing LLC. Suing together shows a litigation alliance, not common ownership, and the complaint's allegations against the defendants are contested and are not findings. What the caption shows is that a dealer, a management company, and three leasing entities chose to act together in one case. Whether they operate as one group is not established by the caption.

The addresses run both ways

Documented The New York and Los Angeles sides cite each other's addresses. Ariel Tzur's address on the California filings for Tower LA and CarToo is 3110 Queens Boulevard, the dealership. Ralph Tsour's address on the 2023 Florida filing is the same. In the other direction, Tower Mobility's public LinkedIn company page has listed the Queens Boulevard address among its locations; that is a company self-description, not a filing, and we have not preserved a dated capture of it. In progress Tzur's 2023 affidavit gives a third address, 1415 Cromwell Avenue in the Bronx, as CB Livery's principal office; the BBB lists a Bronx branch of Tower Auto Mall on West 170th Street, a few blocks away.

A lead on the leasing fleet

Unverified A specialist newsletter on New York's for-hire vehicle market, AutoMarketplace, has published an analysis of TLC plate holdings that tentatively groups Venture Leasing, CB Livery, and QLR entities in a Tower-related fleet cluster. Its authors say their ownership mapping is partly educated inference. We cite it as a lead, not a finding: it points to where TLC license records would show who holds plates on how many vehicles, and that registry check has not been done.

"Tower Group"

Documented A publicly posted professional biography identifies Mai Kruvi as Chief of Staff at Tower Group, based in New York. It says she operates across three affiliated entities spanning fleet leasing, lease-to-own services, and commercial lending; credits her with launching a new entity in Los Angeles, an insurance transition covering about 4,000 drivers, and obtaining a California Financing Law license. The biography names none of the companies. We attribute all of it to her, and we treat the license claim as unverified: we could not identify a licensee, license number, or status in the state's public directories, and the absence of a match is not evidence that no license exists. The 4,000-driver figure is her description of an insurance project's scope, not a Tower EV headcount.

What this means for the Los Angeles lot

The Tzur-managed companies at Mission Road are managed by the president of a business whose documented product, in 2016 and again in 2023, is weekly-payment vehicle financing for Uber drivers. CarToo's published terms say a car can be locked out or shut off for non-payment; its prices and total cost are not published. The associated New York business's full terms were reported once, in one driver's 2016 contract. Whether CarToo's executed contracts resemble it is a question only a CarToo contract can answer.
