Driver Stories

The record they don't keep

Uber, Lyft, and the fleets log every mile, every wait, every dollar. They publish none of it. We are building the record from the driver's side, one shift at a time.

A rideshare driver is the most surveilled worker in the transportation economy and the least listened to. The app knows the route, the idle time, the acceptance rate, the tip, the hour. The company that owns the app decides what to publish, and what it publishes is a brochure. This section exists to print the other side: what a shift actually pays, what a car actually feels like at 150,000 miles, what it means when a depot closes and the transfer comes with a threat.

We publish stories in the driver's own words, edited only for length and clarity, with pay records when we have them. We do not invent composites. We do not publish a quote we cannot attribute to a real driver we have spoken to, even when we print it anonymously.

What drivers in robotaxi cities are already saying

Documented The stories are arriving before we have finished building the place to put them. In Austin, veteran drivers have reported earnings declines of 30 to 40% since Waymo's arrival, and incentive payments in Los Angeles and Phoenix have fallen by more than 64%. Nicole Moore, who drives for Lyft and leads Rideshare Drivers United, has called airport trips many drivers' "bread and butter", and robotaxi access to terminals is the thing they fear most. Those airport runs are exactly where Waymo is pushing next.

Researchers who have followed drivers for years have found something quieter and worse: the belief that "the job will eventually disappear" weakens drivers' motivation to fight for better conditions now. The threat does its work before a single car is replaced. That is the thing this site is built to push back against.

Tell your story

You can tell us as much or as little as you want. We are interested in any of this:

  • A shift, start to finish: hours online, rides, what you took home.
  • Your pay stubs, especially if your employer advertises a number your checks don't match.
  • Changes at your depot: closures, transfers, staffing, vehicle condition.
  • What a robotaxi has done to your earnings, with before-and-after numbers if you have them.
  • What you would want a human-driven future to look like.

How we protect you

  • You choose how you appear: full name, first name, or anonymous. Anonymous is the default.
  • We remove employee numbers, addresses, vehicle numbers, and anything that identifies you from documents before publishing an excerpt.
  • Originals are stored offline. We do not share them with anyone, including other drivers.
  • Nothing goes live until you have read the final text and said yes.
  • Workers in California generally have a legal right to discuss pay and working conditions with each other. We will connect you with worker organizations and legal help if you want it before you decide.

This page doesn't send anything on its own. The button copies your text so you can paste it into an email to truthinmobility@proton.me. Attach pay stubs or photos to that email.
